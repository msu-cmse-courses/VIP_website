Transforming Our Knowing to Empower Nous (TOKEN) Lab

In ancient Greek, nous means intelligence. As artificial intelligence infiltrates life at all levels, we as human beings must utilize our own intelligence to first learn and understand this ever-evolving technology and become able to make decisions by exercising human agency. Here, agency means the performance of intentional action.

The TOKEN Lab studies how we as humans learn and understand technology such as artificial intelligence. The aim is not to take sides or compare artificial intelligence to human intelligence. By examining how humans learn about different aspects of artificial intelligence, we hope to provide data-based analysis that offers insights into how learners understand unfamiliar systems and build skills to judge them. These skills ultimately enable humans to exercise their agency and thrive in the ever-changing technology landscape.

As you might have guessed, the name TOKEN was intentionally selected: “token” plays an essential role in the AI world; at the same time, tokens have served as symbols for many things throughout human history. “Transforming” draws on the famous “transformer” architecture in GPT, where the T stands for transformer.

Issues Involved or Addressed

The first project the lab will work on will be built around students learning data retrieved from a learning platform focused on learners’ agentic AI learning experience. Lab members will learn about research ethics, data handling, including numerical data and text data such as learners’ reflections, research design, academic writing, and more. Here are some guiding questions:

  • How has human intelligence interacted with artificial intelligence?
  • How do we, as human learners, learn about artificial intelligence?
  • What are some concrete challenges learners face when learning about challenging technology? How do learners behave in such situations?
  • In which ways can we build truly interdisciplinary research that combines quantitative data with qualitative data to study humans’ learning behaviors while learning about agentic AI?
  • How can we reduce barriers and encourage learners to use their agency on this subject? What are some stereotypes or narratives that discourage learners from engaging in these topics? For example, “I am not smart enough,” “I was never good at math anyway,” or “AI is evil.”

Methods and Technologies

The TOKEN Lab will operate in phases. In the early phases, we will provide training on:

  • Mixed-methods approaches, both quantitative and qualitative
  • Digital literacy, such as file management
  • AI literacy, including language-model basics and different AI applications
  • Data management, collection, and preparation
  • Research ethics, including IRB processes

Technologies we may use include:

  • Git
  • Markdown for documentation
  • Python

You do not need to have much experience with the research methods and technologies mentioned above.

Areas of Interest

Lab advisors hold strong beliefs in the power of interdisciplinary inquiry. The term “interdisciplinary” might seem too abstract or sound like a buzzword. Yet, lab advisors are here to turn this concept into practice with you.

Whether you are a data science student, an engineer, an Arts and Humanities major, a soon-to-be K–12 teacher, or an advertising management major who tells good narratives, as long as you are open to learning with and from others, you are welcome to apply.

Here are a few areas of interest or majors for reference:

Preferred Interests and Preparation

This lab seeks students who are open-minded and not afraid of breaking disciplinary boundaries. Curiosity and kindness are two things we value greatly in the lab. One must be curious about the world around them and motivated to learn new things. Being able to learn and then explain what you have learned to a diverse audience are skills this lab values.

Since the subject being studied here, artificial intelligence, is infiltrating all areas of our lives across almost all communities, we welcome students from diverse backgrounds to apply. We hope you bring what you learn back to your communities. Examples include first-generation students, students from under-resourced rural communities, and international students.

This team is not about the advisors. It is about you, your learning, and you interacting with others. Ultimately, this is a lab run by humans for humans.

During the first semester, Spring 2027, the lab will focus on establishing a strong foundation for a long-term VIP team. Useful preparation and qualities for this lab include:

  • Commitment to interdisciplinary research and open-mindedness when working with others who come from a different background than yours
  • Willingness to contribute to shaping the lab by building infrastructure, learning, and then training others
  • Ability to think critically and make improvements after providing and receiving criticism
  • Attention to detail, since our work requires care with details
  • Ability to apply logic and articulate your ideas with evidence
